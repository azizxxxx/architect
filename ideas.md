## Chosen Direction: Mediterranean Monolith

### Design Movement

Contemporary Mediterranean modernism interpreted through an editorial architecture portfolio: monolithic forms, natural materials, warm daylight, and the visual discipline of an art book.

### Core Principles

1. **Material before decoration.** Stone, concrete, timber, glass, and shadow create the visual hierarchy.
2. **Editorial asymmetry.** Composition should feel art-directed rather than templated, with offset text, staggered images, and generous negative space.
3. **Quiet confidence.** Motion is slow and intentional; typography is large, precise, and never shouty.
4. **Human-scale luxury.** Every section connects architecture to lived experience, not only to visual spectacle.

### Color Philosophy

Warm ivory provides the calm of a paper stock and Mediterranean light. Charcoal anchors the identity with architectural weight. Soft beige and concrete gray act as mineral neutrals. A restrained oxidized terracotta is the ownable accent: a reference to Tunisian earth, clay, and sun-warmed masonry without becoming ornamental.

### Layout Paradigm

Use a vertical editorial narrative with offset columns, full-bleed image moments, narrow reading measures, and section markers that behave like architectural annotations. Avoid uniform cards and centered stacks; let the page alternate between expansive images and tightly composed text blocks.

### Signature Elements

1. A fine terracotta rule that appears as a section marker and expands subtly on hover.
2. Project metadata set in small uppercase labels with generous tracking, like a drawing title block.
3. A monolithic “F” mark built from two intersecting architectural planes, used in the header, intro, and footer.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like moving through a gallery: slow enough to notice, fast enough to stay useful. Hover states reveal metadata rather than decorative effects. Navigation opens as a calm full-height overlay on mobile. CTAs use a compact arrow motion and a tactile pressed state.

### Animation

The intro uses a 5-second maximum sequence: a slow hero scale, a soft mask reveal, a small upward text entrance, then a clean handoff to the homepage. Page sections enter with opacity and y-translation only. Image hovers use restrained scale and translation. Respect reduced-motion preferences and never animate layout dimensions.

### Typography System

Display headlines use **Cormorant Garamond** in medium or semibold italic/roman for an editorial, architectural voice. UI, metadata, and body copy use **DM Sans** with generous tracking and compact line-height. Headlines are large and sentence-led; labels are uppercase at 0.16em tracking; body copy is limited to 55–65 characters per line.

### Brand Essence

FORMA STUDIO is a Tunis-based architecture and interior design studio shaping contemporary spaces through material, light, and proportion. Personality: **tactile, discerning, composed**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines are declarative and human. CTAs are direct but unhurried. Microcopy is concise, specific, and never salesy.

Example lines:

> Spaces designed around the way you live.

> Start with a conversation. Build with intention.

### Wordmark & Logo

The logo is a compact geometric F made from two offset planes, suggesting a plan drawing, a threshold, and the act of framing a view. The wordmark “FORMA” sits beside it in a restrained uppercase sans, never as a default browser heading.

### Signature Brand Color

**Oxide Terracotta — #B56B4B.** A warm mineral accent that carries Tunisian soil, brick, and late-afternoon light into an otherwise quiet palette.
